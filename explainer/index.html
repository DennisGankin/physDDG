PhysDDG explainer

PhysDDG: how a physics-based mutation-stability predictor was built

This predictor was developed in an exercise to test whether AI agents can run a bioinformatics project: a PI agent managed 29 subagents on 26–27 October 2026. All numbers come from the project's files, rerun or recomputed where noted. Claims the project's own audits withdrew are not repeated here.

PhysDDG takes a protein structure and a list of single amino-acid substitutions and predicts, for each, the change in folding stability (ΔΔG, kcal/mol; positive = destabilising), with every physical contribution shown, in about a millisecond per mutation on one GPU.

For each mutated position it builds the original and new side chain the same way in many possible shapes and scores them with classical force-field energies. It adds statistics from known structures (burial, which amino acids fit where, which backbone angles they tolerate). A weighted sum with 41 numbers fitted to 216,000 measurements gives the ΔΔG. On held-out proteins it ranks mutations better than FoldX, the standard physics-based tool (ρ 0.69 vs 0.63), but below deep-learning predictors (about 0.77).

Four choices make the difference: the wild type is modelled exactly like the mutant; shapes are averaged rather than picked; physics is mixed with statistics and the mix is fitted; and everything is batched on the GPU.

Worked example: four real mutations in 1PSE

Real intermediate values from running the shipped default model on a held-out protein. Across all 1,213 measured mutants of 1PSE, PhysDDG reaches ρ = 0.60, FoldX 0.49.

Pseudocode

predict(structure, mutations, stage=2, mode="xtal"): prot = annotate(load(structure)) # step 1 for each mutated site i (batched on GPU): for aa in 20 amino acids: # wild type treated like a mutant R = library_rotamers(aa, phi_i, psi_i) # step 2: at most 30 shapes U_r = sum_terms E_term(r) - kT*ln p(r|phi,psi) # step 3 if stage == 2: mean_field_repack(site + up to 16 neighbours within 6 A) F[aa], E_term[aa], S[aa] = boltzmann_average(U_r) stats[aa] = env_potential, ramachandran, N-cap, ... # step 4 (PDB-derived) for each mutation wt->mut at i: x = (E_term, S, stats)[mut] - (E_term, S, stats)[wt] (+ burial, cavity) ddg = b + w.x + r[mut] - r[wt] # step 5: 41 fitted numbers report ddg, per-term parts, flags # step 6

Results

Recomputed from the stored predictions: PhysDDG ranks better than FoldX on 19 of 21 domains.
  • Ranking vs FoldX on Megascale: +0.063, project CI [+0.006, +0.120] (a bootstrap I reran gives [−0.003, +0.120]). Direction is convincing (19/21), size uncertain.
  • Scale of predictions: Pearson 0.70 vs 0.60 and RMSE 0.78 vs 1.85 kcal/mol (FoldX clipped to [−3, 8]). Strong.
  • Against the 10-parameter statistics model: +0.044 [0.018, 0.071]. The physics adds a real, modest amount.
  • Other assays: no clear win. FireProt Pearson 0.45 vs FoldX 0.42 (not significant); S669 0.34 and S461 0.55, level with DDGun3D and the 10-parameter model; Domainome 0.42 vs 0.43, and 0.39 vs 0.39 after removing training homologues.
  • Selection caveat: at least 12 evaluations touched test data, 5 of which changed what ships. Treat differences ≤ 0.02 as optimistic.

Limitations and when not to use it

Shown not to work well
  • Forward and reverse predictions agree only at r ≈ 0.24 (KORPM 0.88).
  • Finding stabilising mutations (top-5 precision 0.15–0.23).
  • Buried small→large (0.49 vs FoldX 0.55).
  • pLDDT < 70: no method works; flagged.
  • Crystal benchmarks: no clear gain over the 10-parameter model.
Assumptions
  • Fixed backbone; one structure; single substitutions.
  • Weights from one assay (small domains, 25 °C); other assays' ΔΔG are about 1.7× larger.
  • Megascale structures are AlphaFold2 models, so structure source and assay are confounded.
Not validated / do not use for
  • Generalisation across assays (claim withdrawn).
  • Ligand or metal sites, interfaces, multiple mutations, indels, pH or temperature, disordered regions.
  • Best accuracy on well-folded domains: use deep learning (≈0.77).

Terms

Ideas taken from the literature

CHARMM19 and EEF1 energy parameters (Brooks 1983; Neria 1996; Lazaridis & Karplus 1999). Separately weighted energy terms: FoldX (Guerois 2002; Schymkowitz 2005). Same protocol for wild type and mutant: Rosetta cartesian_ddg, shown to matter on model structures by Valanciute 2023. Rotamer libraries and priors: Dunbrack & Karplus 1993, Shapovalov & Dunbrack 2011 (PhysDDG derives its own from PISCES, Wang & Dunbrack 2003). Mean-field packing: Koehl & Delarue 1994; Kamisetty 2008. Environment potentials and volume terms: PoPMuSiC (Gilis & Rooman 1997; Dehouck 2009). N-cap and helix dipole: Richardson 1988; Doig & Baldwin 1995; Hol 1978; Shoemaker 1987. Unfolded-state mass balance: Rossi 2025. Censoring: Tsuboyama 2023, Tobin 1958, Huber 1964. AlphaFold handling: MAVISp trimming (Arnaudi) and keeping AlphaFold side chains (Vangaru 2025). Splits: ThermoMPNN (Dieckhaus 2024). Three ingredients (environment potential, Boltzmann averaging, censoring-aware loss) came from an AI reviewer agent (TP2). Citations come from the project's manuscript and were not re-verified here.

The final method looks the way it does because of eight decisions, most forced by evidence that contradicted an early assumption. Seven fell within about seven hours on 26 October; the eighth, an audit that reversed the shipped model, came the next night.

The research process

Main agent (PI)

Wrote the brief into PROMPT.md, the first design, gates and every decision (D1–D23). Made the calls that mattered: accept the outside critique, move the bar to the 10-parameter model, choose transfer over home-test gain, retract the joint model. It also declared a gate passed that audit 1 showed failing, and twice shipped a term built on test rows or on 8 rows.

Subagents (29)

Engine (geometry, rotamers, scan, predictor), energy terms, fitting (nine assignments over two days), AlphaFold. Three roles produced the evidence discipline: the design critic and two auditors. Others curated data, ran competitors, and wrote the manuscript and release.

Productive vs circular

Early, cheap, independent checks changed the design each time. Circular: the shipped family flipped from Megascale-only to joint and back; the main ladder script was rerun at least ten times; repulsion-cap variants cost hours and landed in noise. Safety-classifier stops interrupted 16 of 29 agent logs without changing results.

Where humans changed direction. Rarely. The brief fixed the goals ("generalise across assays" is the sentence the PI cites when dropping the best-scoring term). Later messages were logistics: "keep going", "try again" after a usage limit, "are you done?", and release instructions (keep the full git history; remove personal identifiers).

Most of what was learned is about the problem, not the code. Ranked within each category by generality and surprise; links go to where each arose.
